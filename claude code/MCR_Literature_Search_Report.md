## Detailed Findings by Compound Category

### 1. Halogenated Methane Analogs (Proven Inhibitors)

#### Bromoform (CHBr₃)
- **Status:** Extensively published MCR inhibitor
- **Binding Mechanism:** Binds reduced B12; inhibits cobalamin-dependent methyltransferase
- **Effectiveness:** 94% methane yield reduction in beef cattle (50-60 mg/kg DM)
- **Species:** Beef and dairy cattle
- **Key Limitation:** Highly volatile; transient effect; requires stabilization
- **Source:** Seaweed (*Asparagopsis taxiformis*)
- **Literature:** mBio, ScienceDirect, ScienceDirect (2025)

#### Dibromomethane (CH₂Br₂)
- **Status:** Published MCR inhibitor
- **Half-life:** 775 minutes (longer than bromoform's 26 min)
- **Mechanism:** Direct MCR inhibition at 1-2 µM (sigmoidal dose-response)
- **Formation:** Degradation product of bromoform in rumen
- **Literature:** Scientific Reports (2025), Frontiers

#### Iodoform (CHI₃)
- **Status:** Emerging alternative to bromoform
- **Advantage:** Fewer carcinogenic concerns
- **Research:** Metabolism and excretion pathways studied in dairy cows
- **Literature:** Journal of Dairy Science (2026)

### 2. Synthetic MCR Inhibitors

#### 3-Nitrooxypropanol (3-NOP, Bovaer)
- **Status:** Most successful synthetic MCR inhibitor; commercially approved
- **Structure:** HOCH₂CH₂CH₂ONO₂
- **Mechanism:** Binds MCR active site; nitrate group in electron-transfer distance to Ni(I); inactivates MCR by Ni(I) oxidation
- **Effectiveness:** ~30% methane reduction in cows
- **IC50:** Micromolar concentrations
- **Species:** Beef and dairy cattle
- **Regulatory:** FDA approved feed additive
- **Literature:** PNAS (2016), Multiple reviews (2022-2025)

#### Bromoethane Sulfonate (BES)
- **Status:** Research compound; poor in vivo effectiveness
- **Mechanism:** Electrophilic attack on MCR Ni(I); alkylation yields ethylene and sulfite
- **IC50:** 4 µM
- **Limitation:** Negatively charged; cannot cross methanogen membranes (requires >1 mM in vivo)
- **Drug Resistance:** Susceptible to drug resistance
- **Literature:** Peer-reviewed but limited in vivo use

### 3. Natural MCR Inhibitors

#### Salvianolic Acid C
- **Status:** Recently identified potent natural MCR inhibitor (2025)
- **Binding Energy:** -8.2 kcal/mol to F430 active site
- **IC50:** 692.3 µmol/L
- **Mechanism:** Dual action - direct MCR inhibition + microbial community modulation
- **In Vitro Results:** 1.5 mg/g DM significantly reduced CH₄ without affecting fermentation
- **Gene Effects:** Downregulation of mcrA and rnfC
- **Microbiota:** Increased Bacteroidota; reduced Methanobrevibacter
- **Species:** Dairy cows
- **Literature:** Journal of Animal Science and Biotechnology (2025)

### 4. Plant-Derived Compounds with Published Literature

#### Lovastatin
- **Mechanism:** HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor (affects archaeal polyprenol biosynthesis)
- **Effectiveness:** 
  - Sheep: 55% reduction (high dose)
  - Goats: 33-39% reduction
  - Cattle: 37% reduction (low-dose)
- **Previous MCR Studies:** Yes (marked as "Previously_Found" in your CSV)
- **Commercial Use:** Used in fermented substrates as natural methane inhibitor
- **Literature:** Multiple papers in Journal of Animal Science and Biotechnology, Frontiers

#### Polyphenolic Compounds
**Caffeic Acid:**
- Significantly decreases CH₄ in high-forage and high-concentrate diets
- Mechanism: Affects cellulolytic bacterial growth
- Literature: Journal of the Science of Food and Agriculture (2021)

**Ferulic Acid:**
- Significantly reduces CH₄ in in vitro rumen fermentation
- Metabolized by rumen bacteria (B. fibrisolvens, Megasphaera elsdenii)
- Potent antioxidant
- Literature: Multiple sources (Wiley, DOI 10.3390/fermentation)

**p-Coumaric Acid & Chlorogenic Acid:**
- Part of phenolic acid group that reduces CH₄
- Antioxidant and antibacterial effects
- Often extracted from plants like fenugreek

**General Polyphenol/Tannin Findings:**
- Condensed tannins > hydrolyzable tannins for methane reduction
- Molecular docking: Polyphenols bind directly to MCR
- Mechanisms: Hydrogen bonding, hydrophobic interactions with microbial enzymes

#### Essential Oil Compounds
**Eugenol:**
- 30 µL reduces methane in vitro
- Variable in vivo results (1.2 g/day ineffective in mid-lactation Holsteins)
- Mechanism: Likely protozoal inhibition
- Literature: Academia.edu, Journal of Dairy Science

**Cinnamaldehyde:**
- Main active compound in cinnamon oil
- Interacts with bacterial membranes; inhibits Gram-positive and Gram-negative species
- Highest doses decrease VFA, gas, and methane
- Variable by diet composition
- Literature: ScienceDirect, PMC (multiple papers)

**Limonene, p-Cymene, Citral:**
- Monoterpenes in citrus and mint oils
- Moderate, dose-dependent methane reduction
- Minimal fermentation impact
- Specific MCR data limited; need targeted studies

#### Medium-Chain Fatty Acids
**Capric Acid (C10) & Lauric Acid (C12):**
- Published methane inhibition literature
- Mechanisms: Antibacterial, antifungal, antiprotozoal
- K⁺ ion leakage from methanogens
- Direct methanogen inhibition (especially lauric acid)
- Literature: Cambridge Core, Nature, Scientific Reports

**Garlic and Allyl Sulfur Compounds:**
- Allicin, diallyl sulphide, diallyl disulphide
- Variable/inconsistent CH₄ reduction effects
- Mechanisms: Direct archaea inhibition; HMG-CoA reductase suppression
- Garlic oil achieved greatest methane reduction among essential oils tested
- Literature: MDPI, Frontiers, Research Gate
